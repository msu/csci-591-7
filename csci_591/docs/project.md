# Project

## Description

The course project involves three components: a proposal, a report, and a presentation. Students will work in groups of three or four to implement an automated vulnerability discovery technique. This can either be a new implementation of a known technique or an extension to an existing open-source project.

## Submissions

### Proposal

The purpose of the proposal is for you to have a fair idea of what you will be working on for your course project and to ensure you have some familiarity with existing work in the area. It is expected that your actual project may differ from your proposed project.

Prepare a 1&ndash;3 page proposal using LaTeX (e.g., [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com/)) which includes a project title, motivation, summary, and at least three related papers or projects. Additionally, the proposal must list all group members for the project and this group membership will remain the same throughout the project.

A strong proposal should include at least four sections:

1. Introduction
2. Related Work
3. Proposed Work
4. References

The Introduction should state why your project is important and summarize the topic, it should also mention the expected contributions of your project. The Related Work section should discuss existing work and connect that work to your project. For example, is your project a re-implementation of an existing project; or is your project an alternative approach that can be contrasted with this existing work? Your Proposed Work will describe the work which you hope to accomplish through this semester. If your project includes research questions, those should be listed here. Plans for any software and hardware development and plans for any experiments should be described.

The References section will contain the bibliography and list all citations made in your paper. LaTeX and BibTex will automatically generate this for you from a BibTeX file (`.bib`).

### Report
The purpose of the report is to capture and communicate all of the project work performed by your group over the semester.

Prepare a 5&ndash;7 page report using LaTeX which includes the following sections:

1. Introduction
2. Related Work
3. <insert the name of your project here\>
4. Discussion
5. Conclusion
6. References

You may reuse and revise content from your proposal for use in your report.

The Introduction, Related Work, and References requirements are the same as for the proposal. The third section will replace the Proposed Work from the proposal and should describe your project in detail. Your third section should include at least one original figure, and the use of several figures which are used to guide the reader through your project are encouraged. Figures should be referenced in your text, your text should walk the reader through your project by referring to supporting figures. Figures are often used to depict an overall system architecture, workflow, or visualization of experimental data.

The Discussion section should describe what was learned from your course project and mention potential future work. The Conclusion should provide a brief summary of the paper.

Reports will be evaluated according to their adherence to the required format, correct formatting, and readability. Reports should be submitted as a PDF.

#### Source Code

Any source code developed for implementations or experiments should be provided along with your report submission. When submitting a project with source code, please package all source code within a top-level directory in a ZIP archive and place the report PDF at the top-level.

An example ZIP file would be structured as:

- <project-name\>-report.pdf
- src/

### Presentation

The purpose of the presentation is to communicate a specific key idea from your report to the audience.

Prepare a 20-minute presentation. This presentation help the audience understand why your project is important and then dive deep into at least one aspect of your project by using examples from your report.

Please view [Simon Petyon Jones' _How to Give a Great Research Talk_ talk](https://simon.peytonjones.org/great-research-talk/). As SPJ states, your report contains all the details, your presentation is just an advertisement for your report.

From SPJ's _Do's_:

- Present your motivation and get your audience hooked within the first two minutes
- Pick one, specific key idea from your paper and build your presentation around that
- Use lots of examples to help the audience develop an intuition about your key idea
- Be enthusiastic

And from SPJ's _Don'ts_:

- Do not include an outline before you've presented your motivation
- Don't present related work
- Don't include slides of technical details; but be prepared to answer technical questions, consider having backup slides with technical details

Evaluation of the presentation will include the structure of the presentation and the ability to clearly answer questions about your project.

Slides should be submitted as a PDF on Brightspace on the day of the presentation.
